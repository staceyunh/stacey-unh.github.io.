1. Introduction

CompanyUnitedHealth Group Inc. (NYSE: UNH), founded 1974, based in Minnesota, USA.
IndustryUS health care: managed care (health insurance) and health services.
Operates inMainly the United States. About 51 million members globally, around 1 million outside the US (Morningstar 2026).
CompetitorsCVS Health (Aetna), Cigna (Evernorth), Elevance Health, Humana, Centene.

Main activities

UnitedHealth runs two businesses. UnitedHealthcare sells employer, Medicare, and Medicaid plans and earned $344.9 billion in 2025. Optum delivers care (Optum Health), data and technology (Optum Insight), and pharmacy services (Optum Rx, $154.7 billion revenue) (UnitedHealth Group 2026).

Strategy

Short term: a "performance agenda" led by CEO Stephen Hemsley: reprice plans to recover 2025 medical cost pressure (adjusted medical care ratio 88.9% versus 85.5% in 2024), restructure, and divest weaker units. The 2026 outlook is revenue above $439 billion and adjusted earnings above $17.75 per share (UnitedHealth Group 2026).

Long term: expand value-based care, scale AI-enabled Optum services, make pharmacy pricing transparent, and reach net-zero emissions by 2050 (UnitedHealth Group 2026).

2. ESG and the SDGs

EnvironmentalNet zero and renewable electricity

The goal is 100% renewable electricity by 2030 and net zero by 2050. In 2024 renewable sourcing reached 27%, and total emissions fell 2.58% (The Sustainable Innovation 2026). This supports SDG 13 (Climate Action) and SDG 7 (Clean Energy). Critically, a 73-point gap in four years is flagged "at risk", and a 2.58% yearly cut is far below what a 90% reduction needs.

SocialClosing care gaps and home visits

UnitedHealth reports 575 million care gaps closed since 2021 against a goal of 600 million by end-2025, plus 16 million home visits in 2024 (The Sustainable Innovation 2026). This advances SDG 3 (Good Health and Well-being). Critically, "gaps closed" measures activity, not health outcomes, and sits awkwardly beside the care-denial lawsuits in Section 4.

GovernancePharmacy pricing transparency

Optum Rx is eliminating spread pricing, replacing volume-based incentives with fixed per-member fees, and disclosing manufacturer payments (UnitedHealth Group 2026). This supports SDG 3 (affordable medicines) and SDG 16 (Strong Institutions). It is credible because it removes a profit mechanism, but Optum Rx still earned $154.7 billion in 2025, so the real test is whether margins survive.

Verdict: the social initiative has the biggest scale, the governance initiative the most credible reform, and the environmental initiative the weakest delivery.

3. Diversity, Equity and Inclusion

In March 2025 UnitedHealth deleted its DEI pages, replaced them with a slimmer "Culture of Belonging" page, and removed references to employee resource groups, diverse recruiting, and a disability hiring programme (TechCrunch 2025; Fortune 2025). The company said it complies with "existing and emerging" laws (Star Tribune 2025). Its sustainability report still lists DEI as a focus, so the public message is inconsistent.

My view: effectiveness will be low to moderate. Inclusion is easier to claim than measure, and removing named programmes and targets removes accountability. The company gives no public workforce targets I could find, so progress cannot be tested.

Example 1: workforce pipeline (weak)

Dropping campus recruiting, employee resource groups and the disability internship page removes the exact channels that build diverse talent. Without them, representation is likely to stall.

Example 2: health equity accreditation (strong)

Thirty UnitedHealthcare plans earned NCQA Health Equity Accreditation in 2024 (The Sustainable Innovation 2026). External audit makes this credible, so equity in care is better protected than equity inside the company.

Recommendation: publish workforce and pay-equity data with numeric targets.

4. AI and Visualisation

Benefits

1. Efficiency at scale. UnitedHealth reports more than 1,000 AI use cases, with AI agents expected to handle over half of calls and claims tools lifting productivity by more than 20% (BankInfoSecurity 2025). Lower administrative cost directly supports margin recovery.

2. New revenue products. Optum Insight is selling autonomous coding and digital prior-authorisation tools (UnitedHealth Group 2026), turning internal AI into products for providers and payers.

Challenges

1. Legal and reputational risk. A class action alleges the nH Predict tool was used to deny elderly patients' care, with claimed appeal reversals above 90%. Discovery continued in 2026 and the company says the tool does not make coverage decisions (Healthcare Uncovered 2026; Computerworld 2025).

2. Data security. Final Change Healthcare cyberattack costs were $799 million in 2025 (UnitedHealth Group 2026). More AI means more sensitive data in more systems, which raises that exposure.

What I Thought of the Course

Coming into this course, I expected a standard academic walkthrough of corporate strategy: memorising frameworks, analysing balance sheets, and applying textbook models to neat business scenarios. However, the experience completely shifted my perspective. Rather than viewing businesses as isolated entities operating in a vacuum, the course reframed organisations as complex, living systems constantly buffeted by macroeconomic shocks, internal friction, and shifting stakeholder demands. It forced me to move away from rigid, one-size-fits-all solutions and taught me how to navigate the messy reality of strategic ambiguity, where there is rarely a single "correct" answer.

What Surprised Me

The biggest eye-opener was the paradox of seeing UnitedHealth's revenue surge by 56% while its profit before tax fell by 34%. Top-line growth is instinctively associated with overall business health. Unpacking this anomaly revealed the hidden dangers of scaling inefficiencies and severe margin compression. It made clear that aggressive market capture and rapid expansion can easily mask ballooning operational costs and poor unit economics. Seeing how a company can sell vastly more while its profitability shrinks shattered my assumption that revenue growth is a reliable proxy for sustainable success.

How the DEI and AI Findings Challenged My Beliefs

The findings on Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) and Artificial Intelligence fundamentally challenged how I view organisational value creation. Previously, I looked at DEI largely through a compliance or HR lens. Examining concrete data suggested otherwise: homogeneous teams suffer from strategic blind spots, whereas genuine cognitive and demographic diversity acts as a risk-mitigation tool and innovation driver. Similarly, looking at AI forced me to stop viewing technology merely as an efficiency tool for automation. The data showed how disruptive AI is to core business models, workforce structures, and competitive advantage, requiring companies to reinvent how they deliver value rather than just doing old tasks faster.

What Was Difficult

The steepest learning curve was reconciling contradictory data points: balancing hard financial metrics with qualitative variables like stakeholder sentiment, market volatility, and cultural factors. Cutting through the noise to find the root cause of strategic failures, when the numbers pointed in opposite directions, was mentally demanding. Moving away from looking for a quick fix and embracing multi-layered root-cause analysis required a total shift in how I approach problem-solving.

What I Will Do Differently and How This Shapes My Career

Moving forward, I will change how I analyse business problems. Instead of taking surface-level performance metrics such as top-line revenue at face value, I will dig deeper into structural costs, unit economics, and second-order effects. This cements my commitment to becoming a more holistic, systems-thinking professional. Whether I step into operational roles, business analysis, or strategic planning, I am now better equipped to handle data-driven complexity, evaluate technological disruption critically, and balance financial reality with human and ethical considerations.
